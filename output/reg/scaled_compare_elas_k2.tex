\textit{Notes:} Each row reports the coefficient on the number of active coal mines and the coefficient on coal production from separate regressions that differ only in the endogenous regressor. The first five panels report two-stage least squares estimates with utility and state $\times$ year fixed effects, where the regressor is summed over watersheds within two flow steps upstream of the utility's intake and instrumented with the interaction of a post-1995 indicator and average upstream coal sulfur content; production is annual. The last panel reports ordinary least squares estimates of mean measured concentration within two flow steps upstream with utility and HUC02 $\times$ year fixed effects, where production is annual. Standard errors clustered at the utility level in parentheses. The first two columns divide the dependent variable and each regressor by its sample mean, so coefficients are elasticities evaluated at the sample means. The last two columns regress the dependent variable divided by its sample mean on the natural logarithm of one plus the regressor, with production measured in millions of short tons. Ratio is the mines elasticity divided by the production elasticity. The equality p-value tests that the two elasticities at the means are equal, using a joint regression clustered at the utility level. *** p$<$0.01, ** p$<$0.05, * p$<$0.1.
